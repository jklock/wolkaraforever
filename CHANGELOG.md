# WolkaraForever 1.0.0

World of Warcraft on an iPad: the client, your UI, your add-ons and your voice, in
one app. The engine underneath is Tolkara; this is the WoW layer on top of it.

## The home page

One screen, four managers: **Game Edition**, **Add-On Manager**, **Voice & Input
Manager**, **Performance & UI Manager**. Choose the client (Forever Beta, Classic
Era, Classic, Retail, Retail Beta), the download region and the language, then
press Play.

The page always says where it is: starting, running, or closed. Rows that would
change something are grey while they can't be used, gold when they can. Quit the
game from its own menu and the page says the game closed and offers to close
WolkaraForever, because a launch can only run one game session.

## Installing and updating

Updates check themselves, resume where they stopped, verify what they download,
and skip files that haven't changed. Play unlocks when the client is ready, and
your settings and add-ons survive the update.

When something is damaged, repair fixes it, including a broken client index.

You choose how the game runs: the developer service, local signing, or an
external JIT tool. The choice is made in the app and can be changed later.

## Add-ons

The installed list searches, disables and restores, pins a version, and removes
packages. It only ever removes what it installed itself.

Import a single archive from Files, open the add-on folder, or browse CurseForge
the way the site does it: artwork, name and current file, downloads, popularity
rank, last update, category, game version and author. Sort by any of seven fields
and tap again to reverse it, filter by game version or category, and choose which
columns to show — it remembers. Download Mac-Ever in one tap.

## Your UI, kept safe

Back up `WTF` and `Interface` to a verified zip. Restore from any backup, with a
snapshot taken first so a restore can itself be undone. Reset the graphics
settings without touching accounts, bindings, SavedVariables or anything else.

Two switches: the in-game gamepad UI, and a performance overlay with frame rate,
frame time, thermal state, battery and memory.

## Playing on a screen with no mouse

A toolbar over the game holds the cursor, the keyboard, the gamepad, settings and
Leave game. The touch trackpad scrolls with two fingers and drags when you hold
and slide. The on-screen pad has rings around both sticks, a layout editor, size
and opacity controls, a grid and haptics.

There is a keyboard of our own for the times iPadOS won't show its own one,
including the `@` an e-mail address needs. It has a microphone key, so dictation
is one tap away instead of being hidden on a keyboard that isn't on screen.
Typing goes into the game's focused field, and the draft box lets you write a line
first and then replace that field with it.

## Controllers and voice

Any button on any controller, extra buttons included, with an inspector that
reports what a pad actually sends. Map a button to talk — hold it, or tap to start
and stop — test the mapping before you go in, and choose whether to submit, add a
trailing space, teach it the names and terms you use, and keep the game from
seeing the key. The transcript lands in whatever field the game has focused.

## Getting out

Some screens, the login screen among them, have no quit of their own. Leave game
closes WolkaraForever cleanly through a short countdown page, which also has a
button in case the countdown doesn't run. Switching apps doesn't stop the game.

## Look and feel

Our own icon, one palette throughout, and an illustrated guide.

The game's own executable is never modified or re-signed — it is loaded as
verified data, exactly as Blizzard shipped it.
