### Callback ownership

`attachMouse:` used to install `mouseMovedHandler` and all three `pressedChangedHandler`s on
`GCMouseInput`. The AppKit bridge installs the same three properties
(`platform/translation/AppKit/AppKit.m`, `installMouse:`), so whichever ran last won: an
input-device refresh could leave the inspector's no-op motion handler in place and the game
received no movement until the next mouse button edge.

The inspector now observes the profile's `valueDidChangeHandler`, which the bridge does not
use, and reads the button's own `value`/`isPressed`. Attribution is explicit through
`AKCIReport(weakSelf, @"mouse", name, …)`, so the removed "mark the device first" comment is
not a lost fix — every report carries its device as an argument.

Proof, `wolkaraforever/tools/test_voice.sh`:

```text
iPad Pro 13-inch (M5)  VOICE PASS: 352 checks   (MOUSE_CALLBACKS PASS)
iPhone Duo             VOICE FAIL: 4 of 352     (MOUSE_CALLBACKS PASS; the 4 are R-017)
```

The regression attaches the inspector twice, sends every button through both cycles and
checks that the host's motion and button handlers still fire, that each edge is observed
once, and that an uninterested inspector publishes nothing while still not stealing the
host's callbacks.

### The tracking pump, in patch 0047

`ak_configuringTouchControls` became `ak_needsTrackingEvents`: the touch-controls editor, the
on-screen keyboard, **or** a held left/right/middle button. The pump now runs *before* the
guest queue is scanned, and a blocking `nextEventMatchingMask:` wait is replaced by default
plus tracking turns while that is true. Idle polls stay nonblocking, so gameplay with nothing
held is unchanged.

Why the pre-queue pump is the fix, not a refinement: the loop returns a queued event before it
ever reaches the pump, so with any event traffic the tracking mode never ran — a held drag
updated only on release.

### Two defects found in review and fixed, not carried

   controller.** `-[AKTouchGamepad refresh]` closed the surface whenever
   `hasPhysicalController && _overlay.configuring`, and `refresh` runs for keyboard, window and

   panel could vanish while it was being used; in the fixture it made the queue-cycle test
   fail about one run in five on the iPhone Duo (`surface=1 firstConfigure=0

   needs no platform patch.

### Risks

- With a button held, `nextEventMatchingMask:` no longer blocks: it services default and
  tracking turns and returns. A guest that holds a button through a long idle could therefore
  spin at up to ~500 polls/s instead of blocking. Bounded by the held-button window, and the
  alternative is the release-only behaviour this fixes.
- A controller that arrives without either a connect notification or an intervening
  `refresh` is not seen as newly arrived. The notification is what the overlay observes, so
  the window is a same-run-loop race, not a steady state.
